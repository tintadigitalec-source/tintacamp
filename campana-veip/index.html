The Need

Young adults increasingly turn to vapes to cope with stress, but the calm a vape delivers is largely relief from nicotine withdrawal: when nicotine drops, irritability and anxiety return, and the cycle repeats.

45%

of young people who vape in Colombia say they do it because of stress or anxiety (UMB surveys).

37.9%

of Colombian university students have tried vaping, and 12.1% used it recently (Colombian Drug Observatory).

1.1%→2.6%

past-month e-cigarette use in Mexico, population aged 12–65, from 2016 to 2025 (ENCODAT).

81%

of young people aged 15–24 who vape say they started to reduce stress, anxiety or depression, and 90% of those who quit feel less stressed (Truth Initiative, United States, 2021, as an international reference).

The campaign runs in the weeks before year-end, when stress peaks across the region: work and study deadlines, gift debts, family gatherings and social comparison.

This social media campaign will aim to close one key gap: young adults believe vaping relieves their anxiety, while the evidence shows it sustains it.

Campaign Overview

This social media campaign addresses the misperception that vaping relieves anxiety, at the time of year when young adults feel it most.

Meet Veip: peace of mind in convenient ten-minute doses.

Veip is a fake vape brand that promises peace of mind and charges it back in anxiety. Its name is “vape” written the way young Latin Americans pronounce it, so everyone gets the joke at first sight.

Key pieces move from sarcasm to an educational turn: after the joke, a “but seriously” segment explains what is really happening, with data, and offers constructive recommendations. Every piece closes with the same message:

¿Todavía crees que vapear te quita la ansiedad? Si te sientes mal, busca ayuda profesional. La tranquilidad no viene en un vape.

(Do you still believe vaping takes your anxiety away? If you are not feeling well, seek professional help. Peace of mind doesn’t come in a vape.)

The campaign follows the EQUIP model of the UNODC toolkit, in three acts:

  • Meet Veip (Engage): the fake brand launches with highly shareable sketches.
  • Life with Veip (Question, Undermine, Inform): the sketches question the belief that vaping relieves anxiety, expose how the industry targets young people, and explain the nicotine-withdrawal cycle.
  • December without Veip (Persuade): real alternatives to manage year-end anxiety, and the invitation to seek professional help.

Audiences and Platforms

Primary audience: young adults aged 18–24 (university students and first jobs).

Secondary audience: adults aged 25–34 (year-end work pressure).

No content or targeting will reach anyone under 18.

Countries (proposed): Ecuador, Colombia, Peru, Mexico, Chile and Bolivia.

Platforms

  • Instagram: reels and carousels, organic and paid.
  • Facebook: paid reels.
  • TikTok: organic reels.

Perception Change Goal

The campaign-exposed audience will score at least 5 percentage points higher than the control audience (no campaign exposure) in disagreeing that “vaping relieves anxiety”. The poll will run December 16–20, 2026.

Both groups answer the same short paid poll in the same countries and age groups: the exposed group is drawn from people who saw or engaged with campaign content, and the control group excludes them.

Campaign Specifics

Type: a five-week social media campaign, from November 9 to December 15, 2026, followed by the evaluation poll on December 16–20.

Online strategy

Pre-test: before filming, the first scripts are tested with 8–10 young adults from the target countries, to check that the humor lands, nobody feels judged and the message comes through.

Organic + paid publishing: two or three pieces per week. Pure sketches (30–60 seconds) run as paid ads, and longer pieces with an explanation segment run organically.

Monitoring + improving: weekly review of impressions and engagement per piece, moving budget to what performs best.

Moderation: daily comment moderation to remove harmful or misleading content, encouraging anyone who expresses distress to seek professional help.

Ad policy safeguards: Veip is always clearly satirical, the device is stylized and never glamorized, and every piece carries the closing message.

Review with UNODC: each piece is shared 48 hours before publishing; if there are no comments, it is published on schedule.

Content Format, Messengers and Schedule

Format: comedy reels, reels with a short explanation segment, and carousels, all in Spanish, with burned-in subtitles and neutral Latin American language.

Messengers: Tinta Digital’s director as Veip’s over-enthusiastic saleswoman; actors playing big recognizable concepts (Mental Health, Humanity, Anxiety, Dependence); and a presenter who explains the data. Scripts and data are reviewed by a mental health professional.

Annex 1: Tone and Style

The pieces borrow formats young Latin Americans share every day: fake commercials, “European mom vs. Latina mom”, bank tellers, TV panels, a royal audience. Big recognizable concepts become characters.

We doWe never do
Laugh at Veip and its promiseLaugh at the person who vapes
Use “we”: we all go through DecemberShame, blame or lecture
Show real alternatives: talking to someone, sleep, movement, professional helpJoke about mental health conditions, medication or seeking help
Put the data inside the joke, with its sourceUse fear or death as the punchline
Show Veip as clingy and too good to be trueMake the vape look cool or aspirational
